# Review of the Capsule Audit + Independent Pass

Date: 2026-07-24
Reviewer: Claude (Fable 5), verifying `AUDIT-2026-07-24.md` and `capsule-mvp.md` against `docs/capsules/*`, `capsule-skill.md`, `skills/architecture-review.md`, `skills/code-standards.md`, and the kit's existing tooling (`scripts/scope.mjs`, `llm-workflow.config.json`).

## Verdict

The audit's mechanical findings are mostly right and cheap to fix. Its two structural contributions — the observed/shadow level and task-scoped work packets — are genuine improvements; adopt both. Its framing overreaches (novelty strawman, five-level ladder, research-grade pilot), and its MVP plan is scaled for a paper, not this team — and rebuilds organs the kit already has. Net: the capsule design survives with corrections; the MVP plan should be cut down hard and folded into the kit.

## Adjudication of the audit's blockers

**A — contract-first vs generated `provides`: CONFIRMED as a doc gap, overstated as a dichotomy.**
The two "pick one" options collapse in any stack with a declaration-first idiom — which is all of them: a typed stub in TS, an interface in Go/Java, a trait with `todo!()` bodies in Rust, a Protocol/stub in Python, OpenAPI/proto at network edges. The authored artifact is the public-surface declaration in the stack's native form; `provides` generates from that surface *before* implementation exists. Contract-first = declaration-first; the contract diff is the declaration diff. One explicit paragraph in design.md §2/§7 resolves it. (The audit's own "use the strongest native representation" is this same resolution.)

**B — `signed.at: <commit>` self-referential: CONFIRMED.** Real mechanical bug; open-questions Q5 already suspected it ("in CONTRACT.md (churny) or a sidecar ledger"). Fix: sign content hashes only (surface hash, contract-suite hash — both tree-addressable), store the attestation outside tracked files (CI artifact, git notes, or sidecar ledger). Resolves Q5.

**C — imports ≠ capabilities: CONFIRMED in direction, conclusion overshoots.**
Rename `channels` to *observed side-effect inventory*; never use security language for it. But "capability language only with a sandbox" ignores the design's actual cited use — review routing, the Chrome model, which also doesn't prevent misuse; it prices review. The practical middle the audit missed: an **I/O funnel lint** — capsule code may not touch network primitives, environment, storage, or ambient globals directly; only platform wrappers may. Every stack enforces this pattern with its native lint (ESLint restricted imports/globals, golangci-lint depguard, clippy disallowed methods, ArchUnit, import-linter). Channels then derive from wrapper call sites, and hiding a channel means visibly breaking a lint — same trust level as every other gate here. Sound enough for review routing; not a security boundary; say both.

**D — semantic test relaxation not mechanically classifiable: CONFIRMED.**
"Relaxing → major, mechanically" is wrong as written. The sound subset survives: contract-test deletion/modification is mechanically *flaggable* (forces acknowledgment + consumer-closure run); *classifying* the change is judgment against intent. Adopt the lightweight version of the audit's fix: an `id` per prose invariant, contract tests reference the IDs they evidence, a diff reports which IDs lost evidence.

**E — four grains conflated: HALF-CONFIRMED.**
The design never claimed runtime/fault isolation — the audit counts its absence as conflation while also noting it's absent. Two grains were genuinely conflated, not four: work allocation and authority. The valuable correction: **permanent one-agent-per-capsule → task-scoped work packets** (mission graph). Keep per-capsule *ownership* (who signs contract changes); compute *allocation* per task, allowing packets to group or split capsules. This also defuses the real nanocapsule pressure the audit identified: if capsule = unit of parallelism, agents get sliced small to parallelize, producing shallow modules.

**F — pilot can't attribute: CONFIRMED for claim 1's wording, replacement protocol REJECTED.**
Pilot claim 1 is comparative ("faster than single-agent conventional") with no comparison arm — fix the wording or add the cheap baseline (tasks run before enforcement *are* the baseline). But the audit's replacement — three conditions, matched task pairs, randomization, adjudication panels — violates the kit's own law: measurement heavy enough to get skipped measures nothing. pilot.md's pre-written kill criteria already are lightweight pre-registration. Adopt only: shadow-first observation (nearly free), held-out acceptance cases for the regeneration drill, count-don't-instrument for the rest.

## Where the audit itself fails

1. **Novelty strawman.** prior-art.md's headline already states every mechanism is battle-tested somewhere and only the combination is unoccupied. The audit "refutes" a claim the design didn't make, and its prior-art table substantially re-derives the design's own steal-map. Genuine narrowings worth keeping: SEMV/tested-semver as prior concept for tests-in-versioning; Spring Modulith named as closest; "wholly unoccupied" → "unoccupied as an assembled, named practice."
2. **The five-level ladder repackages the design's own qualification logic** (Q6, the skill's qualification gate, §9's per-capsule day-one value) with added ceremony. Three states suffice here: **folder → observed → enforced**. "Contracted" is just enforced-at-an-independently-evolving-edge (rare in a monorepo); "sandboxed" is out of scope for a Vite app — accepting that is Blocker C's fix anyway.
3. **No cost accounting for its own additions.** Intent-ID registries, evidence archiving, promotion tests, four-graph data models, adjudication — the audit cures R1 (architecture theater) in the design and reinfects the pilot with measurement theater. Every addition it proposes should pass the same evidence gate it demands of contract fields.
4. **Added human gates throughout** (promotion approval, weakening authorization, human-reviews-until-track-record). The kit's premise is agents-reviewing-agents with human review reserved for high-risk surfaces and contract intent. Each new human gate needs incident evidence, same as new frontmatter fields.
5. **It under-engages the actual thesis.** The audit judges a module architecture for today's repos ("most modules stop at Level 1 or 2") — true, and beside the point of the fleet-coordination bet, which the design's R6/R7 already price honestly. Both hold: build Level 1–2 now; keep the appreciation argument on paper as the documented bet.

## capsule-mvp.md specifically

- **It rebuilds kit organs.** The plan stands up `module-workspaces/` with its own repository-config schema, six commands, and an adapter abstraction — while the kit already has `llm-workflow.config.json` (module map → verify commands) and `scope.mjs` (changed files → radius), and design.md §3 already specified feeding graph facts into scope.mjs. Level 1 is: extend the existing config with per-module entries (root, publicEntries, allowedDeps, verify), run dependency-cruiser, render CONTRACT.md, teach scope.mjs to consume the graph. One or two kit scripts, no parallel universe.
- **The adapter interface (Nx, Bazel, Spring Modulith, import-linter "later") is a premature abstraction — in code.** Generality is mandatory, but it lives in the spec: stack-agnostic invariants, channel categories, contract format, observed/enforced semantics. The code starts with one concrete adapter and extracts the interface when the third stack shows up (rule of three). A spec that never names a stack plus one working adapter beats a speculative adapter framework.
- **Twelve fixture repos + analyzer fault-seeding before any proven value** is gate-weight this team will route around. Keep: fail-closed on empty analyzer output, deterministic output, staleness refusal. Cut the rest until the brief proves useful.
- Stray Codex-ism: "Use apply_patch for edits" in the loop prompt — the plan wasn't localized to this repo's tooling.
- What the plan gets right and should survive any cut: generated-view/authored-policy lifecycle split, fail-closed adapters, conservative impact ("never convert 'not analyzed' into 'not affected'"), no accept-all command, isolation-level honesty enum, and Stage 1's stop question.

## Independent findings (neither document has these)

1. **Shared mutable state is the under-reported channel class — in every stack.** Two modules on one store, global, or table have a behavioral edge the import graph under-reports, regardless of language or state manager. The invariant is stack-agnostic: cross-module shared-state edges are declared with direction (read vs write; the cross-module *write* is the dangerous one). Derivation is adapter work: frontends can often derive it statically from store usage, backends from schema/table ownership (two modules writing one table = no boundary), and a stack with no derivation support still works — those edges are authored and flagged unverifiable. The core spec defines only the channel categories and read/write semantics; it never names a state manager.
2. **Stringly edges (query keys, event names, storage keys) become graph edges with the same funnel trick as authority:** lint string literals out; keys must be imported constants from platform. Until then, report them as a named blind spot.
3. **Regeneration is the least valuable, most-discussed part of both documents.** The practical form — rewrite as a normal PR with the contract suite as guardrails — needs zero new machinery and works today. Keep the drill as a stretch experiment; stop letting it size the design.
4. **The "independent evaluator" at a four-dev startup is a different model given the original issue text and no session context** — the kit's cross-model audit, formalized. That staffs the held-out oracle for ~free.
5. **Greenfield vs brownfield pilot is a false choice.** Shadow observation on the reference app costs ~nothing, answers boundary-fit, and produces the module map you need anyway. The greenfield pet project remains the only way to test the parallelism claim — run it second, only if still wanted.

## Round 2 — review of the token-economy additions (2026-07-24, later)

Context: Luis set subscription continuity as a hard constraint; Codex added the usage sections to `CONSENSUS-2026-07-24.md` and rewrote `capsule-mvp.md` around it.

**Verdict: accept.** The reframe strengthens the thesis — R7 ("economics unmeasured — the actual bet") is now the primary metric instead of a parked risk. The invariants are the right ones (single-agent default + admission test, mechanical-stays-mechanical, one review at the widest seam, no background-agent tax, graph never in prompt preamble), the new kill criteria make usage a first-class kill switch, and the plan honestly refuses false precision on opaque subscription weighting. No smuggled scope found.

Three sharpenings, proposed as consensus amendments:

1. **Cache and diff stability: hot files change only when semantics change.** Analyzer digests, tool versions, and provenance belong in the cold machine report — never in the hot `CONTRACT.md` or capsule `AGENTS.md`. A hot brief that churns on every regeneration re-bills context within sessions, invites agents to spend turns diffing meaningless changes, and puts noise in PR diffs. Stable hot prefixes are also what prompt caching rewards. The word budget bounds the brief's *size*; this rule bounds its *churn* — both are needed.

2. **Rank the levers by magnitude; word budgets are guardrails, not the battle.** A 300-word brief is ~400 tokens; one extra spawned agent or review pass is tens of thousands. Order of leverage: (1) agent count, (2) review passes, (3) tool-output volume — where the brief pays for itself by *replacing* foreign reads, easily thousands of tokens each, (4) retries caused by missing context, (5) hot-brief size. Optimization and measurement effort should follow that order. Obsessing over brief size while admitting one unnecessary reviewer is penny-wise.

3. **Keep "spot-check, don't instrument."** The original pilot.md had this right, and the new R7 proxy list drifts toward instrumentation: transcript-derived counts (foreign reads, large tool-output events) have no free mechanical source today, so they'll either get skipped or get counted by an LLM — both failure modes the plan itself forbids. v1 records only what falls out of existing artifacts for free (agents spawned, verify commands run, wall-clock, accepted/rejected, provider usage when visible); transcript-derived counts stay spot-checks on a handful of tasks. Building telemetry tooling is not a v1 deliverable.
